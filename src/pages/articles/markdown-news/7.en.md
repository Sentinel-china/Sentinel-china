In discrete manufacturing and equipment automation, relays are almost everywhere: solenoid valves, contactor coils, indicator lights, buzzers, interlock circuits, signal switching… Whenever electrical isolation and “dry contact” adaptation are involved, relays remain one of the most reliable choices. The problem is that as I/O points increase and machine variants change more frequently, relays are no longer just about “whether they pull in or not”, but gradually become an invisible layer of engineering cost: cabinet space is taken up by bases and terminals, wiring versions multiply, commissioning and troubleshooting requires constant tracing of wires, and it is hard to ensure consistency when components are replaced after delivery.

The value of IO-Link on the sensing side is already very clear, and on the actuation side, we equally need a more standardized way to make originally scattered switching outputs easier to connect, easier to diagnose and easier to expand. Based on this idea, we are launching two new products: the scalable 16-channel DPDT IO-Link relay module **SIOL-TL-16RM2S**, and the corresponding 16-channel expansion submodule **REX-16R2S**. The former is responsible for IO-Link communication and basic outputs, while the latter is used to expand points without changing the communication architecture, allowing relay outputs to also enter a unified IO-Link management system.

## Core Highlights: DPDT + Scalable + IO-Link – Engineering the “Relay Layer”
![](http://image.sentinel-china.com/265412.png)

At the core of **SIOL-TL-16RM2S** is turning relay outputs from “scattered devices in the cabinet” into a standard IO-Link remote I/O device. The module body provides 16 relay drive outputs with a single-point drive capability of **150 mA**, relay coil voltage of **24 V**, and a **DPDT contact structure** for each relay—that is, each relay has two independent changeover contact sets (two NO + two NC), with a contact rating of **4 A 250 VAC / 4 A 30 VDC**. This structure is highly practical in engineering: it can be used for **two-circuit synchronous switching**, for **interlock/selection circuits**, or to reduce rewiring work when NO/NC logic needs to be changed, making the same electrical design easier to adapt to different customer interface habits.

The second highlight is the **“main module + expansion submodule”** architecture, which truly aligns with the most common reality for machine builders: I/O requirements are not always fixed once and for all at the proposal stage. SIOL-TL-16RM2S reserves an **Extend Port** that can be connected to the **REX-16R2S** expansion submodule via a dedicated cable, further extending **16 DPDT relay outputs**. For projects, this means you can use a single IO-Link port to smoothly scale from **16 to up to 32 outputs**, reducing IO-Link master port occupancy and making it easier to reuse the same control structure across different machine variants and future modifications.

The third highlight lies in **details for installation and maintenance**. The relay contact side of both products uses **push-in terminals**, suitable for fast wiring and stable clamping in high-density I/O scenarios; the communication and load power supply of the main module use **cage-clamp pluggable terminals**, simplifying panel assembly and maintenance replacement. Status indication is clearly partitioned: module power **POWER**, communication status **RUN**, load power **POWER_L**, plus per-channel **LED1…LED16**. During commissioning, you can quickly judge whether “communication is established, load power is normal, and outputs are set”, reducing the time spent on “disconnecting–measuring–reconnecting” cycles.

One note: **these two modules are shipped without relays**. In engineering, relay models can be selected according to your own supply chain and certification system. The documentation provides several reference models to help projects quickly select and introduce suitable relays.

## Technical Details and Usage Tips: Clear Key Parameters for Stable Operation
![](http://image.sentinel-china.com/265413.png)

On the IO-Link side, **SIOL-TL-16RM2S** adopts **IO-Link V1.1**, with interface type **Class A**, frame type **TYPE_2_V**, transmission rate **COM2 (38.4 kbit/s)**, and a minimum cycle time of **3200 μs**. The process data is **4 bytes**; when no expansion submodule is connected, the last two bytes are unused; once an expansion submodule is connected, that part of the process data is used to carry the mapping of expansion outputs. The module supports **ISDU (Index Service)** and **Data Storage (DS)**, as well as **data storage locking**. For system integrators, the significance of these capabilities is that the device can not only achieve stable output control via process data, but can also expose device information and execute system commands, making it easier to keep parameters consistent when replacing devices on site.

On the output side, the main module defines a clear boundary for load power supply current: the load power current **must not exceed 4.8 A**. Each drive output of **150 mA** is used for the relay coil; on the contact side, each relay is DPDT, two NO + two NC, with a contact capacity of **4 A 250 VAC / 4 A 30 VDC**. Both products are **IP20**, with an operating temperature range of **-25…55 °C**, and support **DIN35 standard DIN-rail mounting** or **screw mounting**, clearly positioned as standardized relay output modules for control cabinets or electrical enclosures.

On the expansion side, **REX-16R2S** is used as an expansion submodule for **SIOL-TL-16RM2S** (and the same family of expandable relay modules), connecting to the main module through a **Connect Port**. It similarly provides **16 channels**, **150 mA per channel** drive current, **24 V coils**, DPDT contacts with the **same contact capacity**, and **LED1…LED16** output status indicators. The connection port uses a **box header (IDC)** and requires a dedicated cable; the main module documentation also provides example cable solutions, including a **0.2 m 20-core flat ribbon cable** and **custom-length cables**, allowing flexible routing for different cabinet layouts.

Regarding usage tips, relay contacts themselves are responsible for electrical isolation and circuit switching, but **contact life and reliability are still closely related to load type**. For inductive loads and high inrush scenarios, it is recommended in engineering practice to configure appropriate suppression measures according to the load characteristics—for example, freewheeling and snubber circuits for coil loads, and surge suppression on the AC side—to ensure the contacts work stably within their rated range. At the same time, relay selection must match both **coil voltage** and **contact form**; it is recommended to start with the compatible models provided in the documentation for validation, and then include them in the project’s standard component library.

## Application Scenarios: Three Typical Needs that Best Demonstrate the Value of DPDT IO-Link Relays
![](http://image.sentinel-china.com/265414.png)

In **packaging, assembly and conveying equipment**, a common practice is to use PLC transistor outputs to drive intermediate relays, which then switch valve islands or external circuits. When there are only a few points, this is manageable, but as points grow, terminal blocks become crowded, labeling is hard to keep consistent, and troubleshooting efficiency drops. After modularizing the relay layer, a single IO-Link port can carry a large number of output points, with output states quickly visible both at the master side and on panels. The debugging and maintenance path becomes clearer, making this architecture especially suitable for **batch equipment projects that require standardized delivery**.

In **test fixtures and jigs**, the advantages of DPDT are even more direct: two changeover contact sets in the same relay can complete **synchronous switching of two circuits**, or be used for **signal path selection and interlock logic**. Circuit relationships that previously required two relays or complex wiring can now be implemented in a more compact structure, with unified output management via IO-Link process data mapping, which benefits fixture reuse and upgrades.

In **line modification and point-add scenarios**, “scalability” often determines engineering efficiency. Many projects configure only the basic points at the start to get into production quickly, and later add actuators and indicators as processes change. If relay outputs are built as scattered pieces, modification often means adding more bases, rewiring, and even occupying additional master ports. By instead using a **main-module + expansion-submodule** structure, you can expand I/O while preserving the original communication architecture, reducing the scope of system changes and making modifications more controllable.
